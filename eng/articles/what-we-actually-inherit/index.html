What Do We Really Inherit? Genes and Their Limits

Genomic science reveals that heredity is far more complex than we thought

8 min read

For decades the prevailing belief was: genes are destiny. Either "gifted" with the right genes or not. Either "prone" to disease or not. But the genomics revolution of the past two decades has transformed this picture radically.

The Human Genome: Size and Complexity

The human genome contains about 3 billion base pairs of DNA, distributed across 23 chromosome pairs. Remarkably: about 98.8% of this genome is shared with chimpanzees — so what makes us human? The answer lies in a small but profoundly influential fraction of gene expression patterns, not just sequence.

The Epigenome: How Genes Are Modified by Experience

The most important discovery of recent decades is epigenetics — studying how genes are modified by environment and experience without altering the DNA itself. Psychological trauma, nutrition, the emotional climate of childhood — all alter patterns of gene expression.

This means what's inherited isn't just rigid DNA but also the "memory" of ancestors' life experiences encoded above the genes.

Practical Limits of Heredity

Even for traits "strongly inherited" like height and intelligence, genes explain about 50–80% of variation — the rest? Environment, experience, chance, and free personal choice.

Genes write the draft of the human being — but life, faith, and choice are what edit the final text.
Sources: "The Gene" by Siddhartha Mukherjee, "Epigenetics" by Richard Francis, Nature Genetics 2023
