Welcome Rituals for the Newborn: From the Adhan to Global Celebrations

How societies throughout history have welcomed those newly arrived to the world

7 min read

In every civilization, birth is not a private family event alone — it is a social happening that calls for collective rituals reflecting the community's values and its view of humanity and the meaning of life.

The Adhan: First Word in a Muslim Newborn's Ear

In Islam, the first thing a newborn hears is the adhan called into their right ear — a declaration that this human enters the world under God's name and the words of monotheism. Before knowing anything of this world, they know that God is the Greatest. This is perhaps the deepest identity declaration imaginable.

Japan: The First Talisman

In Japan, the newborn receives an "omamori" — a blessed temple amulet — as their first symbolic gift. This tradition reflects the belief that the child needs spiritual protection as they take their first steps in the world.

West African Traditions

The newborn stays home for a full week before being exposed to the outside world — a period of "introduction" to their warm family world before facing the vast cosmos. Then their name is announced publicly in a large communal celebration.

Birth in Arab-Islamic Heritage

In Islamic tradition: the aqiqah on the seventh day, the naming, shaving the hair and giving its weight in charity. Before Islam, Arabs would nickname the newborn based on their most visible quality or what they hoped for them — birth was a tribal event touching the family's honor and wealth.

How a community welcomes its newborns reveals more about its values than almost anything else.
Sources: "Birth Customs Around the World" — National Geographic, "Rulings on the Newborn in Islamic Jurisprudence", "Cultural Anthropology" by Conrad Kottak
